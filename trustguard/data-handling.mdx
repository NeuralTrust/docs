TrustGuard inspects prompts, completions, and tool traffic, so the first question any
security review asks is what it keeps and where. This page answers that for everything
a [collector](/trustguard/concepts/collectors) sends for evaluation.

## Where records live

This is the answer that matters most, and it is decided by your deployment — not by a
setting you can get wrong later.

| Deployment | Where records live |
|---|---|
| **Self-hosted / hybrid** | **Your own infrastructure, entirely.** |
| **SaaS** | The NeuralTrust region your workspace is provisioned in. |

<Note>
**On hybrid, evaluated traffic never leaves your perimeter.** TrustGuard runs inside
your infrastructure and exports records to the OTLP endpoint **you** configure, which on
a hybrid deployment is a collector inside your own network. Prompts, completions, and
tool payloads are written there and nowhere else.

The link back to NeuralTrust carries **configuration only**: the control plane pushes
policy and collector snapshots down, and your data plane acks the version it applied.
Evaluated traffic is not part of that exchange and has no route onto it. If a data
residency or data sovereignty requirement is what brought you to this page, hybrid is
the deployment that satisfies it.
</Note>

On **SaaS**, records live in the region your workspace was provisioned in. That region is
set at provisioning time and cannot be changed from the console, so confirm it with your
NeuralTrust contact before you onboard traffic.

## What is stored

An evaluation produces two kinds of record, and they are not equally detailed.

| Record | What it holds | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| **Finding** | Which detector fired, on which side, what it decided, and a summary of the evidence — the *kind* of thing found and how much of it, such as one masked email address. Never the value itself. | Drives **Activity**, analytics, and [alerts](/platform/alerts). |
| **Request body** | The traffic that was evaluated, with credentials redacted and capped at **1 MiB**. Anything longer is stored truncated. | Lets you see what was behind a finding when you investigate it in **Activity**. |

The second row is the one to plan around: **the evaluated traffic is retained**. Anyone
who can open **Activity** in your workspace can read the prompt behind a finding, for as
long as the retention window lasts. Scope workspace access on that basis, not on the
assumption that only findings are kept.

A finding on its own is safe to share widely — it says an email address was masked, not
which one. The request body is not, which is why they are worth thinking about
separately.

If you consume this data downstream rather than reading it in the console, the
[event schema](/platform/event-schema) documents the exact shape of both records.

## Credential redaction

Redaction runs **before** anything is written, and it is not configurable.

**Credential headers** are replaced with a placeholder, so a collector API key never lands
in storage:

```http
Authorization: Bearer [REDACTED]
```

The same applies to `Cookie`, `Set-Cookie`, `X-Api-Key`, `X-Auth-Token`, and the other
well-known credential headers.

**Credential-looking body fields** are replaced with `[REDACTED]` by key name, at any
nesting depth: `password`, `passwd`, `secret`, `token`, `api_key`, `apikey`,
`authorization`, `credential`, `access_token`, `refresh_token`, `client_secret`, and
`private_key`, plus any key ending in `_api_key`, `-api-key`, or `_apikey`.

**Multipart bodies** are never stored as content. Each part is reduced to its field
name: file parts keep the filename and drop the bytes, and every other value becomes
`[REDACTED]`.

<Note>
Redaction keys off **names** — header names and JSON key names. It is a guarantee about
credentials in well-known fields, not a content filter over free text.
</Note>

## Attachments

Attachments are handled apart from the detectors' view of the body:

- Files attached to a request are decoded for the detectors that read them (today
  the **Document Analyzer**), and the decoded content is discarded afterwards.
- Remote attachment URLs are fetched server-side under a strict SSRF guard — HTTPS only,
  with loopback, private, link-local, CGNAT, and cloud-metadata addresses blocked — and
  the fetched content is not retained.

Details: [Attachments and SSRF](/trustguard/api/evaluate#attachments-and-ssrf).

## Retention

Retention is a **per-team entitlement**, not a single global default. Every stored record
carries its retention plan and an **absolute expiry** stamped when it was written, so a
record written today expires on a fixed date — changing entitlements later does not
retroactively extend or shorten records already stored.

For teams entitled to TrustGuard the default window is **365 days**. Your team's actual
value is the one stamped on the record, so read it in the console rather than assuming the
default: a shorter window can be provisioned on request.

If you need a specific window contractually, or shorter than what is stamped, raise it
with your NeuralTrust contact — it is an entitlement change, not a policy setting, so you
cannot alter it yourself from the console.

## Reducing what gets stored

If your threat model calls for storing less:

- **Deploy hybrid.** The strongest control available, and the only one that removes
  NeuralTrust from the picture entirely: records are written to, and stay in, your own
  infrastructure.
- **Scope your collectors.** Traffic that never reaches a collector is never evaluated and
  never stored. Attach policies deliberately rather than workspace-wide.
- **Use gates to waive traffic.** A gate with the **Skip** action stops evaluation for
  matched requests, so no detector runs on them and no record is produced. See
  [Policies](/trustguard/concepts/policies).
- **Shorten retention.** Ask your NeuralTrust contact to provision a window shorter than
  the 365-day default.
