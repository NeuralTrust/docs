Applications are for things your team built. The **Employee portal** is for people.

Every gateway has one. A person opens it, installs what they are allowed,
connects their own accounts, and their agents reach those tools through one fixed
MCP URL — the same catalogue, from a client instead of a browser. On a SaaS
gateway it also lists the models they can call, and gives them one **personal
key** for their models and their tools.

Neither governs applications and applications do not govern it. An application's
surface is what an admin bound; a person's Employee portal is what they installed, bounded
by their [Access](/trustgate/access/overview) level.

## Who opens it

| Who | What they get |
|---|---|
| The **Employee** role | The portal and nothing else. No product dashboards, no console — it is the role for the rest of the company. |
| TrustGate **Viewer** and up | Their own portal, alongside the console. |
| TrustGate **Admin** | Theirs, plus a preview of anyone else's ([seeing what someone else sees](/trustgate/access/overview#seeing-what-someone-else-sees)). |

People enter with their NeuralTrust login or your identity provider. From code
or a client that cannot sign in, a person uses their own
[personal key](#personal-key) instead — it opens their portal and nothing else.
An application key cannot open it: the portal is a person's, and a key that runs
as an application is refused.

## What a person sees

Two sections, **MCP Catalog** and **Models Catalog**. A Hybrid gateway has no
models through the portal, so there it is the MCP Catalog alone. The
**Personal key** button in the header serves both.

In the **MCP Catalog**, the filter beside the search switches between **All** —
everything they may install, each card showing whether it is installed, waiting
on their account, or outside their access — and **My access**: what they hold,
with the state of their own account on each, and a **Connections** list of the
servers that want a personal account.

Opening a server shows what it does, how many tools it brings, and one **Next
step** line — connect, reconnect, finish setup, request access, or nothing to do.
That line is the whole instruction; there is nothing else to read.

The **Models Catalog** is covered in [Models](#models) below.

## Installing

Installing a catalogue server creates or reuses **one registry entry per
catalogue code** on the gateway. What is per person lives elsewhere: their
account on a forwarded-auth server, vaulted under their identity, and any
per-user setup values a server's URL is built from.

An install answer is a state, not a success flag:

| State | What it means | What happens next |
|---|---|---|
| **Installed** | Tools are on the Employee portal's MCP URL now. | Nothing. |
| **Connect** | The server needs the person's own account. | They open the connect link and sign in once. |
| **Needs setup** | The server's address is built from values only they know — a subdomain, a workspace. | They supply them on a hosted form. Secrets are entered only there, never through the tool. |
| **Choose an instance** | An admin connected several instances. | They pick one. |
| **Request access** | The server is outside their selection. | They write why, and it goes to [Approvals](/trustgate/access/overview#requests-instead-of-refusals). |
| **Needs admin setup** | The server wants a shared credential only an admin can add. | An admin finishes the registry entry. |

The same states name the badges in the portal: **Installed**, **Connect**,
**Reconnect needed**, **Pending**, **Needs admin setup**.

## Disconnecting an account

A person can take back an account they linked. On the server, under
**Connections**, **Disconnect** deletes the account TrustGate holds for them:
its tools stop working for them until they connect again, and the server stays
installed. A server that uses one account shared by everyone shows no
Disconnect — that account is an administrator's, and is disconnected from the
registry.

## Models

The **Models Catalog** has a card for each LLM provider the gateway has, and one
for each provider of TrustGate's catalog it has not connected yet. Each says
what it serves (Chat, Embeddings, …) and where the person stands:

| State | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Connected** | They can call it now — all its models, or the ones their level names. |
| **Pending** | They asked; an admin has not decided yet. |
| **No access** | Outside their level. **Request access** asks for it. |

Opening a provider they have shows **How to call them**: the base URL, a model
picker, and a snippet for the language they pick. Any OpenAI-compatible client
works — point it at the base URL and send the personal key.

**Request access** asks for one provider, with a reason in the person's own
words, and lands in [Approvals](/trustgate/access/overview#requests-instead-of-refusals)
next to the MCP requests. Approving gives them that provider's models, all of
them. A provider the gateway has not connected yet can be asked for too: the
admin connects it, then approves.

Which models a person reaches follows their [Access](/trustgate/access/overview#models)
level, and it is enforced on every call: a model outside it is refused before
the gateway calls the provider, however many other people use it.

## Personal key

One key per person and gateway, for their models and their MCP tools, on a SaaS
gateway (a Hybrid one has none). The **Personal key** button in the header
creates it, rotates it or revokes it:

- It is shown **once**, when it is created or rotated. Afterwards only its first
  and last characters are, and losing it means rotating it.
- It lasts up to **90 days**. A rotation keeps what the key reaches; the old
  secret stops working at once.
- **Use it** has a ready snippet for the TrustGate SDK, the OpenAI SDK and an MCP
  client.

The key runs as its owner, with what Access grants them — never more. It opens
their portal at `/store/mcp` (send it as `X-AG-API-Key`, `x-api-key` or
`Authorization: Bearer`) and their models through the gateway, and nothing an
application holds. From code, the [TrustGate SDK](/sdks/trustgate/people) takes
it as is. An admin sees whether someone has one in **Access**, and can revoke it
there.

## From an MCP client

Everything a person installs is on one fixed URL — `/store/mcp` on the gateway's
MCP host, the same address for everyone. Pointing Claude Code, Cursor or any
other MCP client at it runs an OAuth sign-in the first time; a client that only
sends headers uses the person's [personal key](#personal-key) instead. After
that the client sees that person's own catalogue, and a *tools changed*
notification whenever they install, connect or lose a server.

It is an MCP endpoint like any other, so the client also gets the gateway's own
tools — the [inventory](/trustgate/mcp/overview#the-gateways-own-tools), and
these:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `trustgate_store_search` | Searches the whole catalogue, not only what is installed, and says of each hit whether it installs straight away or files a request. |
| `trustgate_store_install` | Installs one catalogue code and answers with the states above — carrying the connect link, the setup form or the request form with it. Called again on a server already installed whose account is missing, it returns the link to connect it. |
| `trustgate_store_uninstall` | Removes one, asking which instance when several are installed. |
| `trustgate_store_models` | Lists the providers and models the person's personal key reaches, and the base URL to call them at. |
| `trustgate_store_request_models` | Asks for a provider's models. The answer is a link where the person writes why; the request is filed when they send it. |
| `trustgate_store_personal_key` | Returns a link to a page where the person signs in and creates, rotates or revokes their personal key. The key is shown there, never to the agent. |

An agent with no tool for what it was asked is meant to search here first.
Installing through the portal is what keeps the new server governed, instead of
the person wiring an upstream MCP URL into their client.

Every request a tool files carries the person's own words. The tools take no
reason: an agent asked for one would write it from its own task and an approver
would read it as the person's, so the link opens a form the person fills in.

The page a connect link opens ends with **Close and go back** to the app the
link came from. Some clients only re-read their tool list when asked to, or when
a conversation starts: if the new tools do not appear, refresh the connector's
tools in the client.

## Open or curated

A gateway's **default access** decides what the Employee portal offers everyone who has no
level of their own:

| Default | Effect |
|---|---|
| **All** | The whole catalogue is browsable and installs instantly. A server with no registry entry yet gets one on first install. |
| **Selected** | Only the shelf and what is granted are browsable; anything else becomes a request. |
| **None** | The Employee portal is closed: nothing to browse, installs are refused. |

A level set on a person or a group overrides the default. The effective level is
evaluated on every request, so a change lands immediately.

Curated is the setting most organisations end at. It is not a refusal of
everything else — it turns "I need this" into a request with a reason attached,
decided by an admin, and approving a group instead of a person is how the same
request stops arriving.

## Reconnecting

A connection is reported as **connected** only while its credential can still be
redeemed. When a provider stops honouring the refresh, the person sees
**Reconnect needed** and the server contributes no tools until they sign in
again. The gateway never replays a refresh token a provider already rejected.

## Instances

Admins add instances from the registry whenever two could differ: one account per
user and one shared, two API keys, two Snowflake schemas. Only a public server at
a fixed address takes none. When several exist the Employee portal asks which one
to install or uninstall, and each keeps its own accounts.

## Where it is

The person's view is a page in the platform — the only page an **Employee** has.
The MCP URL to hand to the company is under the gateway's settings, next to the
LLM and MCP gateway URLs. The personal key is under the **Personal key** button
in the portal's header.
