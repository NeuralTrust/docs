---
title: "Rate limits and budgets"
description: "Two policies cap volume and spend: a request rate over a sliding window, and a token or dollar budget that can reject or downgrade when exceeded."
---

Applies to **LLM and MCP** for the rate limiter; **LLM** for the budget.

## Rate Limiter

Counts requests in a sliding window and refuses the ones over the line. You set a
**limit**, a **window** (`30s`, `1m`, `1h`), and the **Retry-After** the refusal
carries. Refused responses carry the standard rate-limit headers so a well-behaved
client backs off on its own.

**Group by header** splits the counter by a header value — `X-User-Id`, say — so
one policy gives every end user of an application their own allowance. It is the
cheap way to get fairness inside one application without a policy per person.

## LLM Budget

Caps usage over a window, in **tokens** or in **dollars**, and decides what
happens when the cap is reached.

| Setting | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Unit** and **Max** | Tokens (whole numbers) or dollars (fractional), per window. |
| **Time window** | Seconds to days. Anything under a minute is raised to a minute. |
| **When exceeded** | **Reject** the request, or **downgrade** the model to a cheaper one on the same provider. |
| **Per-model limits** | Tighter caps for specific models or families — `claude-opus-*`. The most specific pattern wins. |
| **Counting** | Total, input only, or output only. |

The budget is checked before the request goes upstream, using an estimate, and
the actual usage is charged after the response. In observe mode nothing is
blocked; the decision still appears on the event, which is how you learn what a
budget would have done before it does it.

### Dollars are only as true as the rates

A dollar budget prices tokens the same way [Analytics](/trustgate/operate/analytics)
does: your [contract pricing](/trustgate/registry/models#contract-pricing) on the
registry where you set it, otherwise list price. A budget set before the contract
is entered stops spend at list-price arithmetic, which for a discounted contract
means it stops too early.

### Downgrade, not reject

Downgrading keeps the application working when it hits its ceiling — at
`gpt-4o-mini` instead of `gpt-4o` — instead of returning errors. The target must
be on the same provider. It is the better default for anything user-facing; reject
is for spend that must not happen at all.

## Choosing where it applies

| Scope | Typical use |
|---|---|
| **Gateway-wide** | An organisation-level ceiling nobody can opt out of. |
| **Targeted** | Per-tenant or per-application quotas. |
| **Group by header** | Per end user inside one application, without a policy per user. |

Pair a budget with [smart routing](/trustgate/llm/routing#smart-routing) when the
goal is cost: routing lowers the bill on every request, the budget puts a hard
stop under it.
