title: "Prompt & model controls"
description: "Restrict which models a consumer may call and inject versioned system prompts — model allowlist and prompt template policies in the console."
---

Two catalog policies control **what model** may run and **what system prompt** is injected.
Create them under **Policies** → **Catalog**, or attach them from a consumer.

| Policy (`slug`) | Purpose |
|---|---|
| [`model_allowlist`](#model-allowlist) | Restrict models; optional substitute/default. |
| [`prompt_template`](#prompt-template) | Inject context-bound system prompts and named templates. |

Per-registry **model filters** and **defaults** on the consumer Routing tab are complementary
— see [Model resolution](/trustgate/routing/model-resolution). Use the **policy** when you
want gateway-wide or cross-consumer enforcement with observe/enforce modes.

---

## Model allowlist

**`model_allowlist`**

| Control | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Allowed models** | List or patterns the request may use. |
| **Default model** | Used when the client omits `model` (must be allowed). |
| **On deny** | Reject, or **substitute** a safe model. |

Configure under **Policies** with **Enforce** or **Observe**. Targeted scope can pin the
rule to specific consumers.

---

## Prompt template

**`prompt_template`**

| Mode | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Auto-inject** | Always prepend/append a system prompt for matching traffic. |
| **Named templates** | Versioned templates (e.g. `template://…`) selected by request metadata. |

Templates can use context variables (headers, JWT claims, mustache-style placeholders)
depending on the form fields shown for your catalog version.
