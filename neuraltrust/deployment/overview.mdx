<p className="nt-lead">
  Every NeuralTrust deployment is made of the same three planes. A deployment
  model is only a decision about who runs each one — and that decides where your
  prompts and responses live, what you install, and what has to cross your
  network boundary.
</p>

Read this page once to get the concepts straight. Then pick a
[reference architecture](/neuraltrust/deployment/reference-architectures) and
follow its install guide from top to bottom; each guide is written to be run
in order, with a checklist of what to have ready before you start.

## The three planes

| Plane | What it does | What runs there | In the request path? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Control plane** | Where you author policies, configure gateways, and read dashboards and alerts | The console, the product control planes, analytics | No. Data planes pull configuration from it and keep a last-known-good copy, so traffic keeps flowing if it is unreachable |
| **Data plane** | Where traffic is governed: it proxies LLM and MCP calls, evaluates prompts and responses, and stores raw payloads | [TrustGate](/neuraltrust/deployment/trustgate), [TrustGuard](/neuraltrust/deployment/trustguard), the data-plane API, DataAgent, plus your PostgreSQL and Redis | Yes |
| **Inference plane** | Scores text with the ML detectors behind TrustGuard | The Firewall | Yes, for ML-backed detectors. Stateless: it scores the text and stores nothing |

Two properties hold in every model, and most of the install guides follow from
them:

- **Configuration is pulled, never pushed.** A data plane dials its control
  plane over outbound TLS and keeps serving its last-known-good configuration
  if that connection drops.
- **Raw payloads stay with the data plane.** Prompts and responses are written
  to the PostgreSQL next to the data plane that handled them. What reaches the
  control plane is metadata: timings, verdicts, counts.

## Who runs each plane

| | **SaaS** | **Hybrid** | **Self-hosted** | **Central control plane** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control plane | NeuralTrust, EU or US | NeuralTrust, EU or US | You | You |
| Data plane | NeuralTrust | You | You | You, in several clusters |
| Inference plane | NeuralTrust | NeuralTrust, EU or US — or you, opt-in | NeuralTrust, EU or US — or you, opt-in | NeuralTrust, EU or US — or you, opt-in |
| Raw payloads | NeuralTrust | Your PostgreSQL | Your PostgreSQL | The cluster that produced them |
| You install | Nothing | One data-plane cluster | One cluster with every plane | One central cluster, plus one per data plane |

- **SaaS** — NeuralTrust runs everything. Nothing to install and nothing in this
  section to follow.
- **Hybrid** — you run the data plane, so payloads never leave your cluster;
  NeuralTrust runs the console and analytics. The most common choice, and the
  shortest install. → [Hybrid](/neuraltrust/deployment/hybrid)
- **Self-hosted** — you run every plane in your own cluster, console and
  analytics included. The chart calls this mode `external`.
  → [Self-hosted](/neuraltrust/deployment/external)
- **Central control plane** — a self-hosted control plane that serves data
  planes in other clusters, for data that has to stay where it was produced.
  → [Central control plane](/neuraltrust/deployment/central)

The inference plane is the one plane every model shares by default. Running it
in your own cluster is a single switch, `global.firewall.deploy: true`, and is
what makes an air-gapped install possible — see
[Inference plane](/neuraltrust/deployment/inference-plane).

## Regions

NeuralTrust operates its hosted planes in two regions, **EU** and **US**. Your
tenant is provisioned in one of them, and everything NeuralTrust hosts for you
follows it:

| | EU | US |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Control plane: the endpoints a Hybrid data plane dials | `*.neuraltrust.ai` | `*.us.neuraltrust.ai` |
| Inference plane: the hosted Firewall | `firewall.neuraltrust.ai` | `firewall.us.neuraltrust.ai` |

Credentials are region-scoped: a token issued in one region does not
authenticate in the other. A Hybrid data plane selects its region with one
chart value, covered in [Hybrid → Choose your region](/neuraltrust/deployment/hybrid#choose-your-region).
A self-hosted install has no control plane to choose; its only regional
dependency is the inference plane, and the firewall key NeuralTrust issues names
the region.

## Choose a model

Answer these in order and stop at the first yes.

<Steps>

<Step title="Can NeuralTrust store your prompts and responses?">
  Use **SaaS**. There is nothing to deploy.
</Step>

<Step title="Must payloads stay in your environment, but NeuralTrust may run the console?">
  Use **[Hybrid](/neuraltrust/deployment/hybrid)**. Only configuration requests
  and metadata reach NeuralTrust, and your cluster opens every one of those
  connections.
</Step>

<Step title="Must nothing depend on NeuralTrust SaaS at runtime — or is the cluster air-gapped?">
  Use **[Self-hosted](/neuraltrust/deployment/external)**. Add the in-cluster
  Firewall for no outbound dependency on NeuralTrust at all.
</Step>

<Step title="Do several clusters have to keep their own data, with one console over all of them?">
  Use the **[Central control plane](/neuraltrust/deployment/central)**.
</Step>

</Steps>

Then decide the inference plane separately: keep the NeuralTrust-hosted
Firewall, which is the default and the smallest footprint, unless your policy
forbids text leaving your environment for scoring.

## How every install goes

The guides differ in detail, but they all run the same six stages, in this
order:

| Stage | What you do | Where it is covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Pick the shape | Choose a reference architecture and size the cluster | [Reference architectures](/neuraltrust/deployment/reference-architectures), [Capacity](/neuraltrust/deployment/architecture#capacity) |
| 2. Prepare the cluster | Ingress, datastores, DNS, and the network rules for your model | Your model's guide, [Requirements](/neuraltrust/deployment/architecture) |
| 3. Collect credentials | The registry key; console tokens for Hybrid; the firewall key for Self-hosted | [Console setup](/neuraltrust/deployment/console-setup), [Secrets](/neuraltrust/deployment/secrets) |
| 4. Install the chart | Create the Secrets, write the values file, `helm upgrade --install` | Your model's guide |
| 5. Prove it works | Pods Ready, DNS and TLS from the caller's network, LLM and MCP traffic, telemetry arriving | [Validation checklist](/neuraltrust/deployment/validation) |
| 6. Make it resilient | Climb the availability ladder only as far as you need | [High availability](/neuraltrust/deployment/high-availability) |

## One chart for every model

NeuralTrust ships as a **single umbrella Helm chart**,
[`neuraltrust-platform`](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform),
with one value selecting the topology:

```yaml
global:
  deploymentMode: hybrid # hybrid | external | saas
```

The value `saas` selects the **Central control plane** — a control plane that
behaves like NeuralTrust's hosted one but runs in your environment. It is the
opposite of the hosted SaaS model, where there is nothing to install.

### Compare in detail

| Characteristic | **SaaS** | **Hybrid** | **Self-hosted** | **Central control plane** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Control plane | NeuralTrust | NeuralTrust | Your environment | Your environment |
| Data plane (TrustGate / TrustGuard) | NeuralTrust | Your environment | Your environment | Your environment, in several clusters |
| Raw payloads | NeuralTrust | Your PostgreSQL | Your PostgreSQL | Stays in the cluster that produced it |
| Metadata / analytics | NeuralTrust | Exported to NeuralTrust (OTLP) | Your in-cluster ClickHouse | Exported to your central ClickHouse |
| Firewall (ML detectors) | NeuralTrust | NeuralTrust-hosted by default; your environment with `global.firewall.deploy: true` | NeuralTrust-hosted by default; your environment with `global.firewall.deploy: true` | NeuralTrust-hosted by default; your environment with `global.firewall.deploy: true` |
| SaaS dependency at runtime | Full | Config sync + metadata export + hosted Firewall | Hosted Firewall only (none with an in-cluster Firewall; optional hosted export) | Hosted Firewall only (none with an in-cluster Firewall) |
| Clusters | None of yours | One | One | One central, plus one per data plane |
| Console setup first? | Nothing to install | Yes — each product issues tokens you need at install, the firewall JWT included | No; ask NeuralTrust for the firewall key | No, but your console issues tokens to data planes; ask NeuralTrust for the firewall keys |
| Minimum nodes | — | 2 × 8 vCPU / 16 GiB with the hosted Firewall; 3 × 8 vCPU / 32 GiB with it in-cluster | 3 × 8 vCPU / 16 GiB with the hosted Firewall; 4 × 8 vCPU / 32 GiB with it in-cluster | Central as Self-hosted, plus a Hybrid-sized cluster per data plane |
| `global.deploymentMode` | — | `hybrid` | `external` | `saas` |

### The chart repository

The Helm chart is developed in the open at
[NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform).
These pages are the task-oriented guide; the repository is the reference, and it
is worth a look if you are the person actually running the platform.

| There | What it gives you |
| --- | --- |
| [`values.yaml`](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform/blob/main/values.yaml) | Every setting the chart accepts, with its default |
| [`SECRETS.md`](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform/blob/main/SECRETS.md) | Exhaustive per-component Secret and key contract |
| [`VALUES_SCENARIOS.md`](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform/blob/main/VALUES_SCENARIOS.md) | Worked scenarios and the values that implement them |
| [`docs/`](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform/tree/main/docs) | Architecture contract, sizing, network, observability |
| [`values-*.yaml.example`](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform) | Starting points for each topology |
| [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md) · [Issues](https://github.com/NeuralTrust/neuraltrust-platform/issues) | What changed between versions, and where to report problems |

All of it also ships inside the chart artifact, so `helm pull <chart-ref>
--version <VERSION> --untar` gives you the copy that matches the version you run
— see [Get the chart sources](/neuraltrust/deployment/images#get-the-chart-sources).
