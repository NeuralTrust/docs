<p className="nt-lead">
  A template is one simulation: the message, the page behind its link, and the
  page that explains what the person missed. Start from the catalog; write your
  own when you need something that looks like your company.
</p>

**Phishing › Templates** has three tabs: **Simulations**, **Landing pages** and
**Failure pages**.

## The catalog

TrustForce ships with hundreds of ready-made simulations: mostly email, plus
QR code, chat, SMS and voice. They cover credential theft, invoice and payment
fraud, delivery notices, HR and payroll, document sharing, MFA and app-consent
tricks, AI-themed lures, deepfake and voice-cloning scenarios, and look-alikes
of common vendors. Catalog templates are in English, and nearly all in Spanish
as well.

Each template has a **Difficulty**: **Easy**, **Medium** or **Hard**. Failing
an easy one weighs more on the risk score than failing a hard one.

Catalog templates can imitate a well-known brand's look, but they never send
from that brand's real domain or a look-alike of it. They send from your
[sending domain](/employee-security/phishing/sending-domains).

Click **Send test** on any template to receive it yourself, or **Use in
campaign** to start a campaign with it.

## Write your own

Click **New simulation** and choose **Email phishing**, **SMS phishing** or
**Voice phishing**. QR code and chat templates start from a copy of a catalog
template. For email, the editor has four steps:

1. **Define:** name, **Language** (English, Spanish, German or French) and
   **Difficulty**.
2. **Message:** sender name and address, subject, body and button text, and
   the **Sending domain**: **Automatic**, one of **Your domains**, or a
   **Built-in** domain.
3. **Landing page:** what people see after they click.
4. **Failure page:** what they see after they submit it.

**Red flags** are the warning signs the failure page points out: 2 to 6 per
template. **Replace with AI suggestions** proposes them from the message.
**Personalize with AI** rewrites the message in a chat, for example to
mention your company's tools or tone.

Voice templates have no landing or failure page. Their **Message** step holds
the **Conversation**: the caller's objective, how it behaves, and the
**Maximum call length**.

### Approval

Templates you create or change don't go out until an admin approves them:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Being written. Click **Submit for review** when it's ready |
| **Pending review** | Waiting for an Employee Security **Admin** to **Approve** or **Reject** |
| **Approved** | Can be used in campaigns |
| **Rejected** | Sent back; edit it and submit again |

Campaigns only accept approved templates.

## Landing pages

The landing page is the trap behind the link. Pick a kind, then customize it:

- **Sign-in portal**, **One-time code**, **MFA re-enrolment**
- **Document viewer**, **File download**, **Software update**
- **Payment portal**, **Delivery fee**, **Payroll / HR portal**
- **Survey or prize**
- **App consent (OAuth)**, **Human check (ClickFix)**

Without one, a template uses the **Generic sign-in page**. Landing pages store
nothing people type: see [Nothing typed is stored](/employee-security/phishing/overview#nothing-typed-is-stored).

## Failure pages

The **Built-in failure page** names the template's red flags and links the
lesson, if one was assigned. You can design your own instead, for example to
add your security team's contact details.

## AI personas

Voice simulations use an AI persona as the caller. **Phishing › AI personas**
lists them:

- **Synthetic** personas have a generated voice and portrait.
- **Clone** personas imitate a real employee, such as an executive. They need
  that person's written consent and recordings, collected through a link sent
  to them that expires in 14 days. The person can limit their consent to calls
  only, and withdraw it at any time.
