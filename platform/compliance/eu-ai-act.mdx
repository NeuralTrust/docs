<Warning>
**The obligations are yours, not your tooling's.** The EU AI Act binds the provider
or deployer of an AI system. No product makes you compliant and NeuralTrust does not
claim to. This page maps TrustGate and TrustGuard capabilities to the articles they
produce evidence for, so your compliance team can cite concrete technical controls
inside your own programme. Where the products do not help, this page says so.
</Warning>

Each section names an article, states what it requires in one line, lists the
capabilities that produce supporting evidence, and states the gap. Every capability
links to the page that documents it.

## Article 9 — Risk management system

*Requires an iterative, lifecycle-long process of identifying, estimating and mitigating the risks of a high-risk AI system.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Detector catalog](/trustguard/detectors/overview) | A fixed set of what is looked for across data loss prevention, content security, and agent/MCP security — so the scope of what is and is not inspected is explicit |
| [TrustGuard policies](/trustguard/concepts/policies) | Risk treatment: gates filter before detection, detector rules decide the action, one switch between watching and enforcing |
| [Observe mode](/trustgate/policies/overview#mode) | Measure what a policy *would* do before it does it — the decision is recorded on the event without blocking traffic |
| [Alerts](/platform/alerts) | Detections correlated and deduplicated across both products, forwarded to the channels your operators watch |

**Gap.** This is operational risk management of systems already running. Design-time
risk analysis — training choices, foreseeable-misuse analysis, the risk criteria
themselves — is outside what a gateway and a runtime inspector can observe.

## Article 10 — Data and data governance

*Requires appropriate data-governance practices, in particular for personal data reaching the system.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Data Loss Prevention](/trustguard/detectors/data-loss-prevention) | PII and secrets — API keys, access tokens, JWTs — detected on input and output; the one detector that masks, which is what makes the **Transform** action available |
| [Regex Replace](/trustgate/policies/regex-replace) | Deterministic rewriting at the gateway for values you can write down exactly — an internal hostname, a ticket reference, a key prefix. It rewrites; it never refuses |
| [Credential redaction](/trustguard/data-handling#credential-redaction) | Credential headers and credential-looking body fields replaced before any record is written, at any nesting depth, not configurable |
| [Document Analyzer / URL Analyzer](/trustguard/detectors/content-security) | PII and injection in uploaded files (including scans via OCR) and in pages an agent is asked to read; decoded content is discarded after evaluation |
| [Data handling](/trustguard/data-handling) | Where records live per deployment, what a finding holds versus a request body, and the retention stamped on each record |

**Gap.** Article 10 is primarily about training, validation and testing data. These
products govern runtime traffic and have no visibility into how a model was trained.
Cite them for the personal-data-in-operation half of the article, not the
dataset-governance half.

## Article 12 — Record-keeping

*Requires automatic recording of events over the system's lifetime.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Audit logs](/platform/audit-logs) | Sign-ins, membership and role changes, SSO/SCIM, and TrustGate/TrustGuard configuration — who changed what, retained **365 days**, filterable and CSV-exportable |
| TrustGuard findings | Which detector fired, on which side, what it decided, and an evidence summary — the kind of thing found, never the value |
| Request bodies | The evaluated traffic behind a finding, credential-redacted and capped at 1 MiB |
| [Event schema](/platform/event-schema) | The exact shape of both records for downstream consumption |
| [SIEM forwarding](/platform/alert-integrations) | Alert findings delivered as OCSF Detection Findings into your own retention regime |

**Retention is split by design.** Audit events are kept 365 days (SOC 2 minimum).
TrustGuard records carry a per-team retention entitlement — 365 days by default — with
an **absolute expiry** stamped at write time, so changing entitlements later neither
extends nor shortens what is already stored. A shorter window is an entitlement
change, not a console setting.

**Gap.** SIEM forwarding carries alert findings, not every audit row. If you need the
administrative trail beyond 365 days, plan its export separately.

## Article 14 — Human oversight

*Requires that systems can be effectively overseen by natural persons.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Observe mode](/trustgate/policies/overview#mode) | Policies log what they would have done and let traffic through, so a person reviews before enforcement is switched on |
| [Access requests](/trustgate/access/overview#requests-instead-of-refusals) | Reaching a server outside your selection arrives as a request for a human to grant, rather than a refusal |
| [Alerts](/platform/alerts) and [integrations](/platform/alert-integrations) | Detections pushed to the channels operators actually watch |
| [Break the glass](/platform/break-glass) | The documented emergency path when normal access is unavailable |

**Gap.** There is no human-in-the-loop approval on the inference path — no pending
request waiting for a person before a model call proceeds. Oversight here means
observe, alert, grant access, and revoke. The one approval workflow that exists
([access requests](/trustgate/access/overview#requests-instead-of-refusals)) governs
which servers a person may reach, not individual requests.

## Article 15 — Accuracy, robustness and cybersecurity

*Requires resilience against errors, faults, and attempts to exploit the system.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Prompt Guard](/trustguard/detectors/content-security) | Jailbreaks and prompt injection from whoever is typing, on input and output |
| [Indirect Prompt Injection](/trustguard/detectors/agent-mcp-security) | Hostile instructions in what a tool hands back — and in tool descriptions themselves |
| [Rate limiter](/trustgate/policies/rate-limiter) and [per-tool rate limiter](/trustgate/policies/per-tool-rate-limiter) | Resource-exhaustion resistance, per scope and per tool |
| [LLM budget](/trustgate/policies/llm-budget) and [request size limiter](/trustgate/policies/request-size-limiter) | Spend and payload bounds |
| [Routing fallback](/trustgate/llm/routing#fallback) | An ordered fallback chain and load-balanced pools, so a failing provider does not take the application with it |
| [Guardrail choice](/trustgate/policies/guardrails) | Six inspection options, three of which (TrustGuard, Bedrock, Model Armor) can rewrite content in place rather than only refuse |

**Gap, and it is the one to read twice.** A streamed response cannot be inspected or
blocked as it is written — the words are already reaching the reader by the time a
detector would decide. On a streaming application, a response-side guardrail
**observes; it does not protect**. Put the control on the request leg, or on
non-streamed responses, when it has to stop something.

Two more worth knowing: an OpenAI Moderation policy created in the console scores
everything and refuses nothing until someone sets a threshold, and Azure Content
Safety runs on requests only — it never sees the model's answer.

## Article 26 — Deployer obligations

*Requires deployers to use high-risk systems per instructions, assign human oversight, and keep the logs they control.*

Deployer duties are where these products fit most directly: [policies](/trustgate/policies/overview)
are the documented operational control, [roles](/platform/users) assign who oversees,
and [audit logs](/platform/audit-logs) plus TrustGuard findings are the logs under
your control. [End-user attribution](/trustgate/observability/end-user-attribution)
ties traffic behind a shared key to the person a client was serving.

**Gap.** End-user attribution is [explicitly not an identity
claim](/trustgate/observability/end-user-attribution#not-an-identity) — it is a label
the calling application asserts. Do not present it as authenticated identity.

## Article 50 — Transparency

*Requires that people are informed when they interact with an AI system.*

**Not covered.** Neither TrustGate nor TrustGuard detects or enforces AI-identity
disclosure. Article 50 is met by how you design and label your application, not by
anything on this traffic path.

## Machine-readable mappings

Article identifiers are **not** shipped as tags on findings in the API or console
today. This page is the mapping. If you need article-level tagging in exported
events, raise it with your NeuralTrust contact before you build reporting on it.

## Residency

Where evaluated traffic lives is decided by deployment. On **self-hosted or hybrid**
it stays in your own infrastructure and the link to NeuralTrust carries configuration
only. On **SaaS** it lives in the region your workspace was provisioned in, fixed at
provisioning time and not changeable from the console. If a residency requirement is
what brought you here, confirm the region with your NeuralTrust contact before
onboarding traffic — see [Data handling](/trustguard/data-handling).
