<Warning>
**A certification is yours, not a vendor's.** ISO/IEC 42001 certifies *your* AI
management system — its policies, processes, roles and evidence. No product confers
it, and NeuralTrust does not claim to. What TrustGate and TrustGuard give you is
operational evidence your AIMS can point to and enforcement it can rely on. Where a
clause needs something a gateway and a runtime inspector cannot supply, this page
says so.
</Warning>

This page covers **TrustGate** and **TrustGuard** only. Each row links to the page
that documents the capability, so an auditor can follow the claim to its
configuration. Nothing below is aspirational.

## Clause mapping (4–10)

| Clause | What it asks | What TrustGate / TrustGuard give you | What they do not cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4 — Context** | Understand your AI footprint and interested parties | The [Registry](/trustgate/registry/overview) is the inventory of what a gateway may reach: [model providers](/trustgate/registry/models) on the LLM plane, [MCP servers](/trustgate/registry/mcp) on the MCP plane, each an entry plus the credential that reaches it | Scoping decisions, stakeholder analysis, your organizational context |
| **5 — Leadership** | Policy, roles, accountability | [Roles and permissions](/platform/users) per product, [SSO](/platform/sso) and [SCIM provisioning](/platform/scim), [break-glass](/platform/break-glass) for the emergency path | Writing your AI policy and assigning accountability |
| **6 — Planning** | Risk assessment and treatment | The [detector catalog](/trustguard/detectors/overview) defines what is looked for; [TrustGuard policies](/trustguard/concepts/policies) and [TrustGate policies](/trustgate/policies/overview) are the treatment, each with an **Observe** mode that measures before it enforces | Risk criteria, risk acceptance, the assessment method itself |
| **7 — Support** | Resources, competence, documented information | This documentation set; [audit logs](/platform/audit-logs) and TrustGuard findings as retained documented information | Training your people, competence records |
| **8 — Operation** | Operational planning and control | The gateway is the operational control point: [policies](/trustgate/policies/overview) decide budgets, rate limits, what may be said and which tools may run, scoped by who calls and what they touch; [access levels](/trustgate/access/overview) decide who reaches which server | Processes outside the traffic path |
| **9 — Performance evaluation** | Monitoring, measurement, analysis | [Alerts](/platform/alerts) over TrustGuard and TrustGate telemetry, with correlation and deduplication; [SIEM forwarding](/platform/alert-integrations) of findings as OCSF Detection Findings | Internal audit and management review |
| **10 — Improvement** | Nonconformity and corrective action | Findings carry the detector, the side, the decision and evidence; [policy history](/trustguard/concepts/policies#history) records what changed and when | The corrective-action process itself |

## Annex A controls where the products are direct evidence

| Control theme | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **A.5 — Assessing impacts of AI systems** | TrustGuard findings per policy and detector, with severity and evidence summary. This is operational impact evidence, not a completed impact assessment |
| **A.6 — AI system life cycle** | Request-level records through the gateway; [end-user attribution](/trustgate/observability/end-user-attribution) ties traffic behind a shared key to the person a client was serving |
| **A.7 — Data for AI systems** | [Data Loss Prevention](/trustguard/detectors/data-loss-prevention) detects PII and secrets on input and output and is the one detector that **masks**, making the Transform action available; [credential redaction](/trustguard/data-handling#credential-redaction) runs before any record is written and is not configurable |
| **A.8 — Information for interested parties** | Not covered by these two products today — see the gap below |
| **A.9 — Responsible use of AI systems** | Enforced [guardrails](/trustgate/policies/guardrails), [rate limits](/trustgate/policies/rate-limiter), [LLM budgets](/trustgate/policies/llm-budget) and [request size limits](/trustgate/policies/request-size-limiter), per application and per scope |
| **A.10 — Third-party relationships** | The [Registry](/trustgate/registry/overview) holds every provider and MCP server a gateway may reach, with the credential and who holds it; [Indirect Prompt Injection](/trustguard/detectors/agent-mcp-security) inspects what a third-party tool hands back, and the tool descriptions themselves |

## Where the residency question is decided

Clause 8 and Annex A.7 both turn on where records live, and that is settled by
deployment, not by a setting:

| Deployment | Where TrustGuard records live |
|---|---|
| **Self-hosted / hybrid** | Your own infrastructure, entirely |
| **SaaS** | The NeuralTrust region your workspace was provisioned in |

On hybrid, evaluated traffic never leaves your perimeter — the link back to
NeuralTrust carries configuration only. On SaaS, the region is fixed at
provisioning time and cannot be changed from the console. Full detail in
[Data handling](/trustguard/data-handling).

## What to hand an auditor

- **Inventory** — registry entries for model providers and MCP servers per gateway.
- **Control configuration** — the policy set in force, its mode, and its scope.
- **Operating evidence** — TrustGuard findings; request bodies behind them, credential-redacted and capped at 1 MiB.
- **Administrative trail** — [audit logs](/platform/audit-logs) for sign-ins, role and membership changes, SSO/SCIM, and TrustGate/TrustGuard configuration, retained **365 days**, exportable as CSV.
- **Retention statement** — every TrustGuard record carries its retention plan and an absolute expiry stamped at write time.

## Gaps, stated plainly

- **No ISO 42001 clause identifiers as machine-readable tags.** Findings are not tagged with clause or Annex A references in the API or console. This page is the mapping.
- **A.8 (information for interested parties) is not covered.** Neither product detects or enforces disclosure that a user is interacting with an AI system.
- **Clauses 4, 5, 6, 7 and 10 are only partly supported.** Context, leadership, risk criteria, competence and the corrective-action process are organizational work no tool performs for you.
- **A streamed response cannot be inspected or blocked while it is being written.** On a streaming application a response-side guardrail observes; it does not protect. Put the control on the request leg when it has to stop something. See [Guardrails](/trustgate/policies/guardrails#the-one-limit-shared-by-all-of-them).
- **Credential redaction keys off names**, not content — header names and JSON key names. It is a guarantee about credentials in well-known fields, not a filter over free text.
