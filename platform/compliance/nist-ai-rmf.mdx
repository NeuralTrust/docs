<Warning>
**The AI RMF is voluntary and organizational.** It describes functions your
organization performs, not features a product ships. There is no certification and
nothing to be compliant *with*. This page maps TrustGate and TrustGuard capabilities
to the functions they produce evidence for, so a risk team can cite technical
controls inside its own programme.
</Warning>

The framework is weighted towards governance and design-time work. Two runtime
products land squarely in **MEASURE** and **MANAGE**, contribute usefully to **MAP**,
and touch **GOVERN** only where it becomes operational. That shape is stated below
rather than smoothed over.

## Coverage by function

| Function | What it asks | Where these products land |
|---|---|:--|
| **GOVERN** | Policies, roles, accountability, culture | Partial — the operational half |
| **MAP** | Context, categorization, capability inventory | Partial — inventory, not context |
| **MEASURE** | Analyse, benchmark, monitor, track | Strong |
| **MANAGE** | Prioritize, respond, recover, communicate | Strong |

## GOVERN

*Cultivate a culture of risk management: policies, accountable roles, documented processes.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Roles and permissions](/platform/users) | Who holds which level of access per product |
| [SSO](/platform/sso) and [SCIM provisioning](/platform/scim) | Access granted and revoked through your identity provider rather than by hand |
| [Break the glass](/platform/break-glass) | A documented, auditable emergency path |
| [Audit logs](/platform/audit-logs) | Sign-ins, role and membership changes, SSO/SCIM, and TrustGate/TrustGuard configuration — retained 365 days |
| [Policy history](/trustguard/concepts/policies#history) | What a policy was before it changed, and when |

**Gap.** GOVERN is mostly about accountability structures, legal and regulatory
awareness, workforce competence and third-party governance policy. These are written
and enacted by people. The products evidence *that* access and configuration were
controlled, not *that* your governance is sound.

## MAP

*Establish context and categorize the AI system — what it is, what it touches, what could go wrong.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Registry](/trustgate/registry/overview) | The inventory: every [model provider](/trustgate/registry/models) and [MCP server](/trustgate/registry/mcp) a gateway may reach, with the credential and who holds it |
| [Gateways](/trustgate/concepts/gateways) | Which gateways exist and what each fronts |
| [Detector catalog](/trustguard/detectors/overview) | The fixed set of risks that are inspected for — so the boundary of what is *not* inspected is explicit too |
| [End-user attribution](/trustgate/observability/end-user-attribution) | Which population a system actually serves, behind shared keys |

**Gap.** MAP asks for business context, intended purpose, affected populations and
foreseeable misuse. The registry tells you what is connected; it does not tell you
what the system is *for* or whom it affects. Attribution is
[not an identity claim](/trustgate/observability/end-user-attribution#not-an-identity).

## MEASURE

*Use quantitative and qualitative methods to analyse and monitor AI risk.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Observe mode](/trustgate/policies/overview#mode) | A policy's decision recorded without enforcement — the measurement step before the control step |
| TrustGuard findings | Which detector fired, on which side, what it decided, with an evidence summary |
| [Alerts](/platform/alerts) | Prioritized, deduplicated detections correlated across both products |
| [Event schema](/platform/event-schema) | The exact record shape, for your own analysis downstream |
| [SIEM forwarding](/platform/alert-integrations) | Findings as OCSF Detection Findings into your existing analytics |

**Gap.** MEASURE also covers TEVV of the model itself — accuracy, fairness and
validity benchmarks. That is testing work, not traffic inspection; it belongs to
TrustTest, not to these two products. Neither measures whether a model is *correct*.

## MANAGE

*Act on measured risk: prioritize, respond, recover, communicate.*

| Capability | What it evidences |
|---|---|
| [Enforce mode](/trustgate/policies/overview#mode) | Risk treatment actually applied — block, or rewrite in place where the guardrail supports it |
| [Guardrails](/trustgate/policies/guardrails) | Six inspection options, chosen per risk and per application |
| [Rate limits](/trustgate/policies/rate-limiter), [budgets](/trustgate/policies/llm-budget), [size limits](/trustgate/policies/request-size-limiter) | Bounded consumption per scope |
| [Routing fallback](/trustgate/llm/routing#fallback) | Recovery: an ordered fallback chain and load-balanced pools when a provider degrades |
| [Access levels](/trustgate/access/overview) | Containment — who reaches which server, revocable |
| [Alert integrations](/platform/alert-integrations) | Communication to the channels operators watch |

**Gap.** No human-in-the-loop approval sits on the inference path: there is no pending
request awaiting a person before a model call proceeds. And the recurring limit
applies here too — a streamed response
[cannot be blocked while it is written](/trustgate/policies/guardrails#the-one-limit-shared-by-all-of-them).

## Profiles and tagging

The AI RMF expects organizations to build **profiles** — a selected subset of
outcomes for a given use case. Nothing in either product produces or consumes a
profile, and findings carry no AI RMF category identifiers in the API or console.
This page is the mapping.
